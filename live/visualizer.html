Payments

The bays are shared: the server keeps a pool of ten residents, each with their own wallet, and any car can plug into any of the four bays. A bay's charge point lives on the server and speaks genuine OCPP 1.6J to the CSMS over a loopback socket — it boots, authorizes the car owner's RFID tag against their wallet, starts a transaction, streams meter values stamped with the simulation clock, and stops; the CSMS prices every interval by the tariff band in force at its timestamp and debits that resident's wallet. If all bays are busy, arriving cars park in normal parking and queue; when a session ends, the longest-waiting car takes the freed bay. The world — cars, queue, sessions, wallets — lives on the server, so everyone opening this page shares one world, and rejoining mid-session restores it exactly. A real charger pointed at this server would be billed identically. “Reset day” sends the cars home and clears the ledger; wallets persist.
